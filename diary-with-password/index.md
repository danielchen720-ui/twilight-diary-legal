# Diary with password: the honest version

If you're searching for a diary with password protection, you want one thing: a diary that stays shut unless you open it. Here's the honest version of how Diary BFF does that, including the part that might surprise you.

## It's a PIN, not a long password

Diary BFF locks with its own 4-digit PIN. Not a long password with symbols, and not your phone's passcode.

That's on purpose. Diary BFF is made for writing at night, often in the dark, often with one hand. A long password at midnight is the kind of thing that makes you not bother. Four digits you can type without thinking means you actually keep the lock on.

## What makes a short code hold

A short code is only weak if it can be guessed over and over. So:

- **Wrong tries count down.** Enter the wrong PIN and you'll see how many tries are left.
- **Then it makes you wait.** After too many wrong tries, the diary asks you to wait before trying again, and the wait gets longer.
- **There's no Face ID shortcut.** Diary BFF doesn't offer Face ID or Touch ID unlock, by design. The PIN is the key.
- **It's separate from your phone.** Someone with your phone passcode can't open your diary. Someone who picks up your phone while it's unlocked can't open it either.

## If you forget it

If you forget your PIN, you can start a reset with your phone's own device authentication. For your privacy, the new PIN only unlocks after a 10-day wait, so someone who knows your phone passcode can't use it to open your diary today. Your entries stay intact.

Pick a PIN you'll remember, and don't reuse your phone's passcode. That would undo the whole point.

## What's behind the PIN

A night diary. You write whatever your head is doing, and it stays on the page. When you ask, your diary BFF answers in a few sentences, with at most one question. The replies aren't written by a person; your diary BFF is an AI that lives in your diary. It remembers what you wrote, with the date.

- **Notifications never show what you wrote.**
- **Your entries are not used to train AI.**

## Who it's for

- People who want their diary locked separately from their phone.
- People who share a phone passcode with a partner or family.
- Anyone who tried a lock that was too much effort and turned it off.
