# A voice journal app for the nights you'd rather talk

Some nights, typing feels like too much. You're lying down, the room is dark, and what's on your mind comes out faster than your thumbs can keep up. A voice journal app lets you say it instead.

Diary BFF is a diary you can talk to. You hold to record, say what's on your mind, and it turns into text on tonight's page. You can mix the two: type a line, say the rest, type again. It all ends up in one entry.

## How recording works

- **Hold to record, let go to stop.** There's one button, and it works one-handed.
- **It becomes text on the same page.** What you said joins the entry right where you are, so you can read it back later instead of listening to it again.
- **You can pick the language you speak**, or leave it on Auto.
- **The recording stays with the entry**, so you can still play it back after it has turned into text.

## Where your voice goes

This is worth knowing before you choose a voice journal app, so here it is plainly.

By default, your iPhone's own speech recognition turns the recording into text, and Diary BFF doesn't upload the audio. In Settings there's an option called **More accurate transcription**. It's off unless you turn it on. If you do, the recording is sent to Groq, a transcription service, to be turned into text. The setting says so right next to the switch.

Either way, your entries are not used to train AI.

## Someone writes back, when you ask

A voice note to yourself usually just sits there. In Diary BFF, when you want a reply, you ask. Your diary BFF reads what you said and answers in a few sentences, with at most one question. No lists, and no advice unless you ask for it.

The replies aren't written by a person. Your diary BFF is an AI that lives in your diary. It remembers what you told it, with the date, and once a week, on Sundays, it writes you a letter about your week.

## A lock of its own

Talking out loud is personal, and so is what it turns into. Diary BFF opens with its own PIN, separate from your phone's passcode, with no Face ID shortcut. Someone can be holding your unlocked phone and still not open it. Notifications never show what you wrote, because they can appear on a lock screen.

## Who it's for

- People who think out loud and lose the thought by the time they've typed half of it.
- People who journal in bed, in the dark, with one hand.
- Anyone who wants to say something tonight and read it back next week.
