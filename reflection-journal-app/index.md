# A reflection journal app that reflects back

Most reflection journal apps hand you a question. What are you grateful for? What did you learn today? What would you do differently? The questions are fine. The trouble is that at the end of a long day, a question can feel like one more thing to answer well.

Diary BFF takes a different route. You write whatever is actually on your mind, in your own words. Then, if you want, your diary BFF reflects it back.

## Reflection starts with what's already there

At night, you don't usually need a prompt to know what to write about. It's the conversation you keep replaying, the email you haven't sent, the thing someone said at lunch that you're still turning over. That's the material.

So Diary BFF opens on tonight's page with nothing to fill in. No mood picker, no template, no sign-up first. You start typing. A messy paragraph counts. One sentence counts.

## Someone to reflect it back

Reflection is easier with someone else in the room. When you ask, your diary BFF reads what you wrote and says one thing back: a few sentences, at most one question. It doesn't repeat back what you just said, and it doesn't hand you a plan unless you ask for one. It won't put labels on you.

Sometimes that one line is the thing you couldn't quite see. Sometimes it's a question you hadn't asked yourself. And some nights you don't ask at all, and that's fine too.

## Reflection over time

One night of reflection is useful. A few weeks of it is where patterns show up.

Your diary BFF remembers what you wrote, with the date. If the same worry keeps coming back, it can notice — and when it mentions an earlier night, it tells you which one, so you can go back and read it.

## A weekly reflection, written for you

Once a week, your diary BFF reads back over your nights and writes you a letter about your week: what kept coming up, what changed, the night that went better than you expected. It's a weekly review you don't have to write yourself.

## One page for each night

Your writing, the chat and the replies all stay on one page for that night. A month later, you can scroll back and read a night as it happened, not scattered across a journal in one app and a conversation in another.

## Private enough to be honest

Reflection only works if you write what you actually think. Diary BFF opens with its own PIN, separate from your phone's passcode, with no Face ID shortcut. Notifications never show what you wrote. Your entries are not used to train AI. You can export your diary any time.

## No score to keep

A reflection journal app shouldn't turn reflection into a chore. Skip a night, and it won't bring it up. Come back after a week away and the page is just there.
