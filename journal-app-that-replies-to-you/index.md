# A journal app that replies to you

You've probably tried journaling, and maybe you've wished for a journal app that replies to you. A notebook by the bed, an app with a nice font — maybe it lasted a week. The page is patient, but it's also silent — you pour out the thing that's been looping in your head, and nothing comes back.

A journal app that replies to you fixes the silence. Diary BFF is one: a private night diary where a friend reads what you wrote and says one thing back.

## Why a reply changes things

Writing something down helps because it gets the thought out of your head. But for a lot of people, the part that really lets go is being *heard* — the moment someone says, "yeah, that sounds like a lot," or "you said the same thing about your sister last month."

That's what a reply is for. Not to solve anything. Just to land the thought somewhere so you can stop carrying it.

## How replies work in Diary BFF

Your diary BFF is built to sound like a friend who's awake at 2am, not like an assistant.

- **Quiet until you ask.** It doesn't interrupt while you write. When you want a reply, you ask.
- **Short.** One or two sentences, three at most. At most one question.
- **No lists, no lectures.** It doesn't hand you a plan unless you ask for one, and it doesn't repeat back what you just wrote.
- **It remembers.** It can bring up something from an earlier night — and when it does, it tells you which night.
- **No labels.** It talks like a friend in a diary, not like an expert.

Type it, or chat with your diary BFF. Either way, your words and the replies stay together on one page for that night — a diary, not a chat log.

You can also give your diary BFF a name. Some people do on night one; some never bother.

## Replies that know when to stop

A lot of apps that talk back are built to keep you talking. That's the opposite of what you want at 2am.

Diary BFF is designed to help you wrap up. When you're done, you're done — the thought is on the page, your diary BFF has said its piece, and you can put the phone down. There's no feed to scroll and no inbox filling up.

## A letter at the end of the week

Replies happen in the moment. Once a week, there's something slower: your diary BFF reads back over your week and writes you a letter about it. It might notice the thing you kept coming back to, or the night that went better than you expected. The full Sunday letter is part of the paid version.

## What you don't get

Some things were left out on purpose:

- **Nothing to keep up.** No badges, no check-ins, no "you missed a day." Skip a night, and it won't bring it up.
- **No interruptions.** It doesn't jump in while you write.
- **No sales talk.** Your diary BFF doesn't bring up subscriptions in its replies.

## Private enough to be honest in

A journal that replies only works if you're willing to write the real thing. Diary BFF is locked with its own PIN, separate from your phone's passcode — so someone holding your unlocked phone still can't open it.

Notifications never show what you wrote. Your entries are not used to train AI. You can export your diary any time, and exported backups can be locked too.
