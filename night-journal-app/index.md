# A night journal app, built for the hour after the lights go off

Most journal apps are built for the morning: a fresh page, a prompt, a plan for the day. A night journal app has a different job. It's for the moment you're lying in bed, the room is dark, and your head is still going.

At night, the writing isn't a reflection exercise. It's getting the thing out of your head and onto something that isn't you. Diary BFF is built around that moment.

## What a night journal app needs to get right

### Nothing to decide before you start

At midnight, a blank "New Journal" screen with templates, tags and moods is one decision too many. In Diary BFF, you open the app and you're on tonight's page. You start typing. There's no sign-up first.

### Usable with one hand, in the dark

You're on your side, one arm under the pillow, the screen turned down low. A night journal app should work in that position: big enough to tap, calm enough not to light up the room, nothing that needs two hands.

### Writing that stays writing

At night you don't want a coach. Your diary BFF stays quiet while you write. It doesn't jump in, and it doesn't pop up suggestions halfway through a sentence.

### A reply when you want one

Sometimes getting it out is enough. Sometimes you want someone to hear it. When you ask, your diary BFF reads what you wrote and says one thing back: a few sentences, at most one question, no lists and no lectures. It doesn't repeat back what you just said.

And it remembers. If you wrote about the same thing last Tuesday, it can say so — and it tells you which night it means.

### One page for the whole night

What you write, what you say in the chat, and what your diary BFF says back all live on the same page for that night. A month later, you can scroll back and see the whole night as it happened, not scattered across a notes app and a chat window.

## What night writing looks like

Some nights it's a list: the email you have to send, the thing you forgot to buy, the dentist. Some nights it's one sentence you keep circling. Some nights it's a whole conversation you're replaying, with the better reply you thought of three hours too late.

All of that belongs in a night journal. None of it needs to be good writing. The point is that it's out of your head and somewhere safe, and you can put the phone down.

## Somewhere safe means a lock of its own

Night writing is often the writing you'd least want someone else to read. Diary BFF opens with its own PIN, separate from your phone's passcode, with no Face ID shortcut. Someone can be holding your unlocked phone and still not open it.

- Notifications never show what you wrote, because they can appear on a lock screen.
- Your entries are not used to train AI.
- You can export your diary whenever you like, and the backup can have its own lock.

## And on Sundays, a letter

Once a week, your diary BFF reads back over your nights and writes you a letter about your week — the thing that kept coming up, the night that went better than expected. It's the closest thing to a friend who's been listening all week. The full Sunday letter is part of the paid version.

## No score to keep

A night journal app shouldn't make you feel behind. Diary BFF doesn't count against you. Write four nights one week and none the next, and the app won't bring it up.
